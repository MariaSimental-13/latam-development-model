# LATAM Economic Bloc Simulation

> Exploratory economic model simulating passive vs. active regional integration across Latin America through 2065

**Tools:** Python · Pandas · Streamlit · Plotly · World Bank Data

## Project Description

This project explores the economic potential of Latin America under a hypothetical regional integration model, analyzing how different levels of cooperation could impact long-term economic growth across the region.

The analysis compares two scenarios — **passive integration** and **active integration** — projecting regional and country-level GDP trajectories through 2065 using World Bank data.

---

## Analysis Scenarios

### Passive Integration
Countries maintain their current individual development trajectories with limited levels of regional cooperation.

### Active Integration
A strategic investment model where leading economies reinvest in regional infrastructure, manufacturing, education, and connectivity — generating stronger compounding growth across the bloc.

---

## Project Objectives

- Model the economic potential of a unified Latin American trade and investment bloc
- Compare passive vs. active regional integration scenarios through 2065
- Analyze GDP, GDP per capita, poverty, and education indicators across multiple countries
- Build interactive country-level economic profiles and regional projections
- Visualize multi-country comparative data through an interactive dashboard

## Key Findings

- Brazil and Mexico represent the two largest economies by GDP within the region
- Under active integration, regional GDP projections show significantly stronger growth through 2065
- Education and poverty indicators vary substantially across countries, reflecting key structural differences
- Smaller economies show proportionally higher relative gains under active integration scenarios
- A unified bloc could reduce structural disparities between leading and emerging LATAM economies

---

## Methodological Considerations

This project is an **exploratory simulation** based primarily on historical economic growth trends and available structural indicators from the World Bank.

Current projections do not account for complex variables such as:

- Population growth and demographic aging
- Inflation and monetary policy
- Political stability and institutional quality
- Public debt and fiscal constraints
- Climate change and environmental factors
- Migration and labor mobility
- Disruptive technological transformation

The model continues in development with the objective of integrating additional economic, social, and demographic variables to build more robust and multidimensional regional simulations.

---

## Research Question

**What would Latin America's economic trajectory look like under a unified regional integration model?**

The data suggests that active regional cooperation could generate significantly stronger growth outcomes across the region — though structural, political, and institutional barriers represent critical variables not yet captured in this exploratory model.
